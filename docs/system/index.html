How Zeroday works

An agent that finds why your debt isn't going down, proposes a plan that grows your net worth, and moves money only when you approve.

How it decides (the money logic)

Goal

Highest net worth (assets − debts) in 24 months. Debt above 8% APR goes to $0 as fast as that allows.

Which debt first

Every debt gets its minimum. Extra money goes to the highest APR. When one hits $0 its payment rolls to the next.

Where money comes from

Monthly surplus → cash above the emergency buffer → CD → brokerage. Never retirement or property. Each source is used only if it beats leaving the money where it is, after tax and penalties.

Spending cuts

Essentials stay whole. A cut needs a month where you already spent that little, and at most 30% off a category.

Safety

  • The agent can only read and propose. Its one write is a pending proposal.
  • The SQL tool runs on an in-memory copy of one customer's data: SELECT only, one statement, time and row limits. Other customers' data isn't there at all.
  • The validator runs on propose and again on approve. Approve runs only once per proposal and refuses if balances changed since the plan was made.
  • Keys live in .env only.

What's real, what's assumed

Unknown outflow. C001 sends about $2,890/mo out of checking with no clear destination. It's the plan's biggest lever, so the agent must flag it and ask the customer before counting it as savings.
  • PNC data has no merchant names and spending is flat month to month, so realistic cuts are small (~8%).
  • Nessie has no balance history. History comes from the PNC CSVs; Nessie is where money actually moves.
  • Linked accounts (student loan, brokerage, store card, car) are generated per customer and stored in SQLite.
